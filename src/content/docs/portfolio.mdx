By the end of Level 2 you should have a portfolio that proves you can do the job, not just that you took courses. Write each piece for a named audience: a SOC lead, a CISO, a detection engineer.

## The core portfolio (Levels 0–2)

- **IOC enrichment tool** (Level 0) — small, documented, on GitHub.
- **Intelligence requirements** for a fictional organisation (Level 1).
- **Threat actor profile** of a regional actor, with sources and confidence (Level 1).
- **ATT&CK mapping** of a public report, with a Navigator layer (Level 1).
- **One-page BLUF assessment** and a 5-minute brief (Level 1).
- **Collection plan** (Level 2).
- **Infrastructure hunting report** with graded pivots (Level 2).
- **Malware triage note** with IOCs, ATT&CK mapping and a YARA rule (Level 2).
- **Critique of a vendor report** (Level 2).
- **Capstone campaign assessment** (Level 2).

## Track pieces (Level 3)

Each track page lists one or two deliverables. Two strong track pieces beat seven shallow ones.

## Publishing safely

- Use only public data and published reports. Never publish client data, internal telemetry or anything shared with you under TLP:AMBER or TLP:RED.
- Defang indicators (`hxxp://`, `example[.]com`) in public write-ups.
- Do not publish live credentials, personal data from leaks, or anything that would help someone attack a named victim.
- Say what you are confident about and what you are not. A portfolio piece with honest confidence levels reads as more senior than one that sounds certain about everything.
- Ask someone experienced to review at least one piece before you publish it.
