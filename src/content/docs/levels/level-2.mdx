## 2. Infrastructure analysis and pivoting

The skill that most separates technical CTI analysts. Start from one indicator, find what else the adversary runs, and grade how sure you are about every link.

<Checklist list="l2.infra" title="Infrastructure" />

<Aside type="caution">
Shared hosting, CDNs and parking pages create false links. One shared IP is a lead, not a conclusion. Write down why you believe each pivot.
</Aside>

## 3. Malware triage for intelligence

You are not a reverse engineer yet. The goal is to get intelligence out of a sample fast: what it talks to, how it persists, what family it belongs to.

<Checklist list="l2.malware" title="Malware triage" />

<Aside type="danger">
Only handle live malware in an isolated lab VM, never on your daily machine or a work network. See [Ethics, OPSEC and law](/cti-roadmap/ethics/).
</Aside>

### Capstone: MuddyWater case study

Use ESET’s report [MuddyWater: Snakes by the riverbank](https://www.welivesecurity.com/en/eset-research/muddywater-snakes-riverbank/) (December 2025). It documents a campaign against targets in Israel with a confirmed Egyptian victim, new tooling, and a joint operation with a subgroup of OilRig.

Produce a campaign assessment that includes:

- A timeline and victimology.
- The attack chain mapped to ATT&CK.
- Infrastructure you can still find from the published indicators, with every link graded.
- How the actor’s aliases differ across vendors.
- Detection opportunities.
- Intelligence gaps and what collection would close them.
- An attribution assessment with confidence, and what would change your mind.
