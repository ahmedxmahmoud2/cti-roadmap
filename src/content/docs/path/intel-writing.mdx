## What you will learn

### Reading reports critically

Most of your input will be other people's reports. For each one, ask:

- What did they observe, and what do they assess?
- What is the evidence behind the attribution?
- How old is the data, and who was the audience?
- What does it mean for *my* organisation or client?

Practise on real reports. [APTnotes](https://github.com/aptnotes/data) collects public APT reports going back years.

### Writing

- **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front):** the conclusion and what the reader should do come first.
- **Estimative language:** words like *likely* and *almost certainly* with agreed meanings, plus a stated confidence level.
- **Audience:** an executive brief, a SOC alert and a technical report are three different documents.
- **Facts vs assessments:** always visible to the reader.

## Project: rewrite a report

Take one long vendor report and write two things from it: a one-page brief for a manager (BLUF, what it means, what to do) and a short technical note for a SOC team (TTPs, indicators, detection ideas). Keep both honest about confidence.

## You are ready to move on when

- You can summarise any report in three sentences without losing the key judgment.
- Your writing separates facts from assessments and states confidence.
