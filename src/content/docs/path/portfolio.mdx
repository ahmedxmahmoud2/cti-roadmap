You are not advanced because you finished courses. By the end of the path you should have real research and projects to show.

## Your research portfolio

- **APT threat actor profile:** a complete profile of one group (steps 1 and 5).
- **Report rewrite:** one vendor report turned into a manager brief and a SOC note (step 2).
- **APT campaign analysis:** one attack campaign from start to finish, mapped to ATT&CK (step 1).
- **Infrastructure hunting report:** discovering and linking adversary infrastructure (step 3).
- **Malware triage note:** one sample triaged, with IOCs and TTPs (step 6).
- **Dark web intelligence report:** analysing underground activity (step 4).
- **Threat intelligence feed:** your collection and enrichment pipeline (steps 0 and 8).
- **Report-to-hunt package:** hunting hypotheses with queries or Sigma rules (step 7).
- **Vulnerability intelligence report:** a CVE linked to exploitation and threats (step 9).
- **Strategic intelligence assessment:** the threat landscape for one sector.
- **CTI platform project:** MISP, OpenCTI and a SIEM working together (step 8).

Publish what you can on GitHub, a blog or LinkedIn. Never publish client data or anything shared with you under TLP:AMBER or TLP:RED, and defang indicators in public write-ups.

## A weekly study system

At 8–10 hours a week:

- 3 hours of technical learning.
- 2 hours reading threat reports.
- 3 hours of practical labs.
- 2 hours of research and writing.

Each week, pick one threat actor or campaign and work on it. For example APT29, then APT28, then Lazarus Group, then compare their campaigns, TTPs, sources and infrastructure. Do not treat group names as fixed: check how attribution and aliases differ between sources.

## You are at an advanced level when you can, without a tutorial

- Collect intelligence from several sources and judge its quality.
- Discover threat-related infrastructure without a ready-made IOC.
- Analyse a campaign and the relationships between its parts.
- Extract intelligence from malware.
- Make an attribution assessment based on evidence.
- Turn TTPs into hunting hypotheses and detection rules.
- Build an automated CTI pipeline.
- Write an intelligence assessment that both technical and non-technical readers can use.

You should also understand sharing restrictions: review [FIRST TLP 2.0](https://www.first.org/tlp/).
